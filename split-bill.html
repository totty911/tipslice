Why a dedicated bill splitter helps

Group dinners stall at the same moment every time: the check is face-down on the table, someone opens a notes app, and three people disagree about whether tax belongs in the tip base. A focused bill splitter removes that friction. You enter one total, a tip rate, and how many people are paying. TipSlice returns a clear per-person share and a breakdown you can screenshot or read aloud.

Unlike a general tip calculator that starts from “one diner,” this page defaults to two people and emphasizes the even split. It still supports sales tax and the choice to tip before or after tax, so the math matches how your group actually settles up.

How to split a restaurant bill fairly

  1. Confirm what is already on the receipt. Look for automatic gratuity on large parties, corkage, or a service charge. If a tip is already included, set the tip field to zero or add only what you want on top.
  2. Decide on even vs. itemized. Even splits work when orders are similar in price. If one person ordered steak and another had soup, each person can run this tool on their own subtotal instead.
  3. Agree on a tip rate before dividing. Settling the percentage first avoids redoing the math when someone prefers 20% over 15%.
  4. Include tax only once. If the printed total already has tax, enter that amount as the bill and leave the tax switch off. If you have a pre-tax subtotal, enter it and switch tax on with your local rate.
  5. Use the rounded share. TipSlice rounds each person’s share up to the next cent so the group never comes up short when paying the restaurant.

Common split scenarios

Friends splitting one check

Enter the food-and-drink subtotal, pick a tip (18% is a solid default in many U.S. restaurants), set the headcount, and send the “share per person” figure in your chat. If someone paid the whole bill on a card, the others can Venmo or cash-app that exact amount.

Couples or roommates

Two-person splits are the default here. Add tax if you are working from a pre-tax menu total. For recurring takeout nights, bookmark this page and keep the tip percentage you already agree on.

Office lunches and team outings

Larger groups often see an automatic gratuity. Read the receipt first. If 18% is already printed, leave tip at 0% in the tool (or enter only an extra amount as a percentage of the bill if you want to add more). Splitting after the fact with a clear per-person number reduces awkward follow-ups.

Rounding and leftover cents

Dividing money by three, five, or seven rarely lands on exact cents. This splitter rounds each person’s total up so cash or peer payments cover the restaurant’s charge. A short note under the results explains when the combined shares sit a few cents above the true total. That small overage is usually easier than asking one person to cover a shortfall.
