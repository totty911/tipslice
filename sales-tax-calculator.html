Sales tax without the spreadsheet

Menu prices, online carts, and handwritten quotes often show the amount before tax. At the register the total jumps. This calculator answers two everyday questions: “What will I pay after tax?” and “If this receipt shows one number, what was the price before tax?” Both modes use the rate you supply—TipSlice does not fetch live jurisdiction rates—so you stay in control of the percentage.

If the next step is a tip, the dollar comparison is the pre-tax versus post-tax guide. After you know the tax line, jump to the tip calculator or bill splitter.

How to use the two modes

Add tax

Choose Add tax, enter the pre-tax price, and type your combined sales tax rate as a percent (for example 8.25, not 0.0825). The tool shows the tax dollars and the total you should expect to pay. Amounts round to the nearest cent.

Remove tax

Choose Remove tax when you only have a tax-inclusive total—common on some receipts, vacation packages, or “tax included” stickers. Enter that total and the same rate. TipSlice backs out an estimated pre-tax price and the implied tax. Minor cent differences can appear versus a merchant’s register because of rounding order; treat the result as a close estimate.

Finding your rate

Type the rate from the receipt, or from the state revenue page for that address. This calculator does not download a rate. Four large states publish the state piece and a local lookup:

  • California Department of Tax and Fee Administration — the statewide rate is 7.25%. In most areas, local district taxes add 0.10% to 2.00%, and more than one district tax can apply. The page links an address lookup. Rates on that page are effective October 1, 2026.
  • New York Department of Taxation and Finance — the state rate is currently 4%, plus any city, county, or school-district rate. Taxable sales inside the Metropolitan Commuter Transportation District add 0.375%. The page links the Jurisdiction/Rate Lookup by Address.
  • Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts — the state sales and use tax is 6.25%. Local jurisdictions can add up to 2%, for a maximum combined rate of 8.25%. The page links the Sales Tax Rate Locator by address.
  • Florida Department of Revenue — the general state sales tax rate is 6%, with listed exceptions, and many counties add a discretionary sales surtax. The department's Address/Jurisdiction Database looks up the sales tax and surtax for a Florida address.

Other states publish their own lookups. If only part of a cart is taxable, run the calculator on the taxable portion alone.

Tax and tipping

Many people tip on the pre-tax food and drink total. The full comparison is the pre-tax versus post-tax guide.

Not tax advice

Use it for a personal estimate. It is not a filing tool, not legal advice, and not a substitute for a point-of-sale system or an accountant. Businesses collecting tax should use approved software and the state page for that sale.

Sales tax FAQ

How do I add sales tax to a price?

Multiply the pre-tax price by the tax rate, then add that tax to the price. A $40 item at 8.25% is $40 times 0.0825, which is $3.30 in tax, so you pay $43.30. Choose Add tax, enter the price, and type the rate as a percent, such as 8.25, not 0.0825. The calculator rounds to the nearest cent.

How do I figure out the price before tax?

If you only have a total that already includes tax, divide by 1 plus the rate written as a decimal. A $43.30 total at 8.25% is $43.30 divided by 1.0825, which comes back to about $40. Choose Remove tax, enter the total, and type the same rate. Treat the result as a close estimate, because a cash register can round in a different order.

Do I include sales tax when I calculate a tip?

The Emily Post Institute says it is acceptable to tip on the pre-tax amount. The dollar comparison is the pre-tax versus post-tax guide; if you only have a tax-inclusive total, use Remove tax on this page first.

Why do I have to type the tax rate myself?

This calculator does not download a rate. California, New York, Texas, and Florida each publish the state rate and a local lookup on the revenue pages linked in Finding your rate. Type the rate from the receipt or from that lookup.
