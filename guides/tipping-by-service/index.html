Tipping by service

By Matt Totty, TipSlice · Last reviewed October 19, 2026

Emily Post's chart is the etiquette range for each service below. Pew's August 2023 survey is what U.S. adults said they do. Toast's Q1 2026 figures are card and digital restaurant tips, and cash is not in that set. A charge already printed on the receipt beats all three.

How to use a row

Pick the row that matches the work, not the room. A bartender working a seated dinner is in the meal tip or the contract's service charge, not in a separate per-drink line, unless you are buying rounds directly from them. A delivery fee stays off the tip row. The delivery guide, the salon guide, and the large-party guide are the longer versions. Outside the United States, start with the travel guide, including Mexico and South Korea.

The example links open the TipSlice calculator with b (bill), p (people), and t (tip percent) filled in. Change the bill after the page loads if your check is different. Add sales tax from the receipt with the tax switch, or by adding &x= and the rate. &o=1 tips on the after-tax total. The pre-tax guide explains why the default leaves that flag off. Restaurants, counters, and delivery

The sit-down row is the Emily Post chart's 15% to 20% pre-tax. The Bureau of Labor Statistics lists a May 2025 median of $16.94 an hour for waiters and waitresses, including tips. The cash-wage rule and the longer restaurant cases are in how much to tip at restaurants.

The Emily Post chart says 10% to 15% of the bill for delivery, and $2 to $5 for pizza depending on size and difficulty. A 2025 Emily Post note says around 10%, or $5 or more, for groceries or other daily items, or restaurant takeout. Fifteen percent of $42 is $6.30. Fifteen percent of $18 is $2.70, which is under that note's $5 for those deliveries. Which app fees reach the driver is in tipping delivery drivers.

Salons and spas

The chart lists 15% to 20% for a hair salon and says to ask that the tip be split among the people who served you. Manicures, facials, waxing, and massage are 15% to 20% of that service on the same chart. The $165 example and the barber and hairdresser wage figures are in the salon guide.

Parties and anything already printed

The large-party row is a warning as much as a percentage. IRS Publication 531 uses an 18% charge on a party of eight as its example of a service charge: the guest did not freely set the amount, so it is wages if the house pays it out, not a tip the worker records in a tip diary. Only an extra amount you choose is a tip. Tapping a 20% button on a total that already includes 18% can turn a $240 food bill into a 38% combined charge. Walk through that case in the large-party guide before a rehearsal dinner or a banquet contract.

Hotels, taxis, and flat amounts

Housekeeping, bellhops, and valets are flat amounts on the Emily Post chart. The housekeeper line is $2 to $5 per day, left daily with a note marked for housekeeping. Bellhops are $2 for the first bag and $1 for each additional bag. Valet is $2 to $5 when the car is returned. A taxi is 15% to 20% of the fare, minimally $1, plus $2 for the first bag the driver carries and $1 per additional bag. The $28 example above at 15% is a $4.20 tip.

The same chart lists a concierge at no obligation for answering questions, $5 to $10 for tickets or a restaurant reservation, and $15 for something hard to get. A doorman who carries luggage is $1 to $4.

What the table leaves out on purpose

Federal law sets no required tip-out percentage. Who may share a pool is in tip-out and tip pooling. Country rules are in the travel guide. Type the sales-tax rate from your receipt. The 8.875% figure in the pre-tax guide is only a New York City illustration.

Type a subtotal into the tip percentage table when you want the dollars at several rates before you choose.
