Tipping by service

By Matt Totty, TipSlice · Last updated October 17, 2026

Use this table to pick a range, then use the linked guide when the situation is not the simple case. Amounts for restaurants, delivery, salons, bars, and hotels follow the Emily Post Institute's general tipping chart unless a row says otherwise. They are U.S. customs, not statutes. A line already printed on your receipt beats the table. Outside the United States, start with the travel guide instead of this page.

How to use a row

Pick the row that matches the work, not the room. A bartender working a wedding as part of a seated dinner is in the meal tip or the contract's service charge, not in a separate $2-per-drink habit, unless you are buying rounds directly from them. A stylist who also sells you a $38 bottle is tipped on the haircut, not on the bottle. A delivery "service fee" never moves a row from the fee column into the tip column.

Restaurants, counters, and delivery

The sit-down row is the one with a real wage story behind it. The Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the May 2025 median for waiters and waitresses at $16.94 an hour, including tips, with the bottom tenth under $8.70. Department of Labor Fact Sheet #15 allows a federal cash wage of $2.13 an hour when tips make up the rest of the $7.25 minimum, and requires the employer to cover any shortfall in the workweek. Several states require the full state minimum in cash before tips. That is why this row is firmer than the coffee row. The long version is how much to tip at restaurants.

Delivery uses two numbers because a percentage alone fails on a cheap order. Fifteen percent of $42 is $6.30, so the percentage is enough. Fifteen percent of $18 is $2.70, so a floor of about $5 is the better tip. The Institute's chart says 10% to 15% for delivery and $2 to $5 for pizza depending on size and difficulty. A 2025 Institute note says about 10%, or $5 or more, for grocery and takeout delivery. Use the higher of the percentage and a few dollars, and add more for a hard trip. Details and the fee labels are in tipping delivery drivers.

Salons and spas

Fifteen to twenty percent applies to the service price. The chart says to split a salon tip among the people who served you. If you do not know whether the shampoo assistant is included, ask the desk before you pay one person and assume the house will sort it. Owners are not excluded on the current chart. Retail is excluded because it is not the service. A spa service charge of 18% or 20% that is already mandatory replaces the row, rather than stacking on it. The salon guide has the $165 cut-and-color example and the May 2024 BLS medians for barbers and hairdressers.

Parties and anything already printed

The large-party row is a warning as much as a percentage. IRS Publication 531 uses an 18% charge on a party of eight as its example of a service charge: the guest did not freely set the amount, so it is wages if the house pays it out, not a tip the worker records in a tip diary. Only an extra amount you choose is a tip. Tapping a 20% button on a total that already includes 18% can turn a $240 food bill into a 38% combined charge. Walk through that case in the large-party guide before a rehearsal dinner or a banquet contract.

Hotels, taxis, and flat amounts

Housekeeping, bellhops, and valets are flat amounts on the Emily Post chart, so a percentage calculator is the wrong tool. Leave $2 to $5 per night for the housekeeper each morning, with a note that says the cash is for housekeeping. A single envelope at checkout often misses the person who cleaned the room on the earlier days. Bellhops are $2 for the first bag and $1 for each additional bag. Valet is $2 to $5 when the car is returned. A taxi or rideshare fare does take a percentage: 15% to 20%, with a minimum around $1, plus the bag amounts if the driver handles luggage. The $28 example above at 15% is a $4.20 tip.

The same chart lists a concierge at no obligation for a simple question, $5 to $10 for a reservation, and about $15 for something hard to get. A doorman who carries bags is in the $1 to $4 range. Ask the desk if the hotel pools or forbids tips.

What the table leaves out on purpose

Federal law sets no required tip-out percentage. Who may share a pool is in tip-out and tip pooling. Country rules are in the travel guide. Type the sales-tax rate from your receipt. The 8.875% figure in the pre-tax guide is only a New York City illustration.

Type a subtotal into the tip percentage table when you want the dollars at several rates before you choose.
