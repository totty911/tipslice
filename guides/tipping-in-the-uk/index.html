Tipping in the UK

By Matt Totty, TipSlice · Last reviewed October 19, 2026

A tip in Britain is not expected the way it is in some countries. VisitBritain says staff must be paid at least the National Minimum Wage, and that you can decline an optional service charge. If nothing was added and the service was good, 10% to 15% is well received.

Is a service charge mandatory in the UK?

Not when the bill presents it as optional. HM Revenue & Customs, in guidance E24, says a service charge is an amount added to the bill before it is presented. It is a voluntary service charge if it is made clear that the amount is purely discretionary and there is no obligation to pay. HMRC accepts that presentation when the literature the customer sees matches what staff say. A charge that is not purely discretionary, where there is an obligation to pay, is a mandatory service charge.

VisitBritain uses the diner's version of the voluntary case: an optional service charge is sometimes added, it is noted on the bill, and you can choose whether to pay it. Visit London says you can ask for a service charge to be removed if you are unhappy with the service, and that tipping is not mandatory. Those visitor pages do not replace E24. If the bill does not say the charge is optional, E24's mandatory category is the one that fits, and this page will not relabel it.

E24 also separates the tax treatment. Tips are outside the scope of VAT when genuinely given freely. A restaurant service charge that customers are required to pay is part of the price of the meal and is standard-rated for VAT. A charge the customer has a genuine option to pay is not, in HMRC's words, consideration for the meal, even if it appears on the invoice. That is a tax classification. It is not a suggested tip percent. E24 does not print one.

What does the tips code require?

The code of practice on fair and transparent distribution of tips says it applies to England, Scotland and Wales, and that the code and specified provisions came into effect on 1 October 2024. It was issued under the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023. Its purpose is the fair and transparent allocation of tips, gratuities and service charges, including both automatically added and other voluntary service charges. The code calls all of those "tips" for short.

The code restates what employers must do. They pass qualifying tips and service charges to workers without deductions, except in very limited scenarios such as deduction of income tax. When the employer controls or significantly influences the distribution, it has to be fair and transparent, and the employer must have regard to the code. Where tips are paid at a place of business on more than an occasional and exceptional basis, there must be a written policy, available to workers, in plain language. Allocation records are kept for three years from the date the tip was paid, and workers can request access. Distribution is due, at the latest, by the end of the month following the month the customer paid. The code's example is a tip left on 23 June, which must be distributed by 31 July.

How the money arrives does not decide whether it is qualifying. A card payment, an app, or a QR code that the employer receives is an employer-received tip. A cash tip the worker keeps, with no employer control, is outside the Act and the code. The Act applies to workers, including eligible agency workers. It does not apply to self-employed people. Employers must have regard to the code. The foreword says failure to observe it fully is not, by itself, proof that an employer acted unfairly. This page is not employment advice. The code is the document a workplace dispute uses.

The code page does not say it applies in Northern Ireland. This guide does not invent a Northern Ireland tipping statute to fill that gap. VisitBritain's visitor note on whether to tip is written for Britain and is the tourism sentence used below.

Do tips count toward the minimum wage?

No, for pay periods that start on or after 1 October 2009. E24 says the right to the National Minimum Wage is statutory, and that tips, gratuities, service charges, or cover charges paid by customers do not count toward National Minimum Wage pay when the employer pays them to the worker. VisitBritain's line that staff must be paid at least the minimum and do not have to live on tips is the visitor-facing version of that rule. It is a different structure from the U.S. federal tip credit in the restaurant guide.

UK tipping FAQ

Is a service charge mandatory in the UK?

HMRC says a service charge is voluntary when it is clearly presented as entirely optional and there is no obligation to pay. It is mandatory when there is an obligation to pay. VisitBritain says an optional restaurant service charge is something you can choose not to pay.

What does the tips code change?

The code of practice on fair and transparent distribution of tips applies to England, Scotland and Wales and came into effect on 1 October 2024. Employers must pass qualifying tips and service charges to workers, keep a written policy where tips are more than occasional, and keep allocation records for three years.

How much does VisitBritain say to leave?

If nothing was added and you are happy with restaurant service, VisitBritain says 10% to 15% is well received. For a taxi it is customary to round the fare up to the nearest pound, and 10% to 15% is a nice gesture if you choose to tip. Visit London's taxi lines do not all use the same percent.
