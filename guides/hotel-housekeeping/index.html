Tipping hotel housekeeping

By Matt Totty, TipSlice · Last reviewed November 13, 2026

This page is the person who cleans the room. It is not the restaurant bill, the porter, or the tour guide. Those have their own pages. The useful problem here is that the published figures do not share a unit. One Emily Post line is dollars per day. A later Emily Post note is dollars per person per day. Québec prints one or two dollars per day. London's visitor page prints a per-night range and, further down, a sentence that does not use that range. Japan and Mexico do not print a dollar amount at all. Adding a U.S. percent of the room rate on top of any of those lines is not what the sources say.

The Emily Post chart and the 2025 note

The Emily Post general tipping chart lists hotel housekeeping as $2 to $5 per day, left daily, with a note marked "Housekeeping - Thank you." The chart does not say "per person." It does not say to leave the whole stay's cash on the last morning only. The service table already carries that chart row. This page does not replace it.

A separate Emily Post article, Etiquette Today: Travel Tipping, dated February 6, 2025, and signed by Lizzie and Dan, uses a different count: roughly $2 to $3 per person staying in the room, and per day of the stay. Leave it each day. Make it clear the cash is for the staff. The article says a note on the pad of paper might read "Housekeeping-Thank you!" and that a couple of crumpled bills on the dresser might be left untouched.

Do not add the two figures together. They are two ways of counting, published by the same institute at different times. For one person on one night, the 2025 note is about $2 to $3, which sits inside the chart's $2 to $5. For two people on one night, the 2025 note is about $4 to $6. Four dollars is inside the chart's daily range. Six dollars is above the chart's $5 top. The chart never says to multiply by the number of guests. The 2025 note does. Pick one document and say which one you are using. This page will not average $2 to $5 and $2 to $3 into a new number.

Cash for the stay, before any percent

Housekeeping cash is addition. The tip calculator's percent buttons are the wrong first step. Multiply people, nights, and the per-person amount, or multiply nights and the per-day amount. The table is that arithmetic and nothing else.

Two guests for three nights at $3 per person is $18. That is 2 times 3 times $3. Leave about $6 each morning if you are following the 2025 note, not $18 on the last day. The chart's reason for a daily habit is the same one the 2025 note states by saying "each day": the person who cleans on Tuesday may not be the person who cleans on Thursday. A single envelope at checkout pays only whoever is assigned that morning, if anyone is assigned to notice it.

Label the bills. The 2025 note's warning is practical: unmarked crumpled bills can be left where they sit because the attendant is not sure they are a tip. A short note with the words housekeeping and thank you is the instruction both Emily Post texts share. Do not leave a stack that is meant to cover several future days in one pile. The note says to leave the tip each day, and a stack that looks like one day's tip can be taken as one day's tip.

Room service is a percent, and it is not the housekeeper

The same February 6, 2025 article treats room service as a different job. It says 15 to 20 percent of the bill before room-service fees and taxes. It says the fee and the taxes are often already on that check, and that the gratuity is separate from them. A $46 food-and-drink line, before those extras, is $6.90 at 15 percent and $9.20 at 20 percent. The totals with the tip, still before the fee and the tax, are $52.90 and $55.20.

Open the $46 line at 15 percent and at 20 percent. If the printed check already folds a delivery charge or a service fee into the number you were about to type, subtract that fee before you use the percent. The article's base is the bill before those charges. The pre-tax guide is the same idea for a restaurant tax line. A resort fee on the room folio is not the housekeeper's daily cash, and it is not the room-service base, unless the folio uses those words for a gratuity. Read the label. The service-charge checker is for a line that is already a percent or a dollar amount, not for a $3 bill on the desk.

Québec

Bonjour Québec says tipping the housekeeper of your hotel room is at your discretion, generally $1 or $2 per day. That is a per-day amount, like the Emily Post chart, not a per-person amount. Four nights at $2 is $8. Four nights at $1 is $4. The same page's restaurant range, 10 to 15 percent of the bill before taxes, is not the housekeeper line. Its note that GST and QST together are about 15 percent is a shortcut for a meal, not a reason to tip 15 percent of the room.

The page also describes a specific tax on accommodations, 3.5 percent of the room price per night, with GST and QST calculated on top, and says that lodging tax is not refundable because it funds regional tourism. That tax is not a tip. Paying it does not replace the $1 or $2. The rest of Canada's restaurant ranges are the Canada guide. Destination Ontario's about page, updated October 28, 2026, mentions porters at $1 or $2 per bag and does not print a housekeeper amount. This page does not invent one for Toronto.

London, and what VisitBritain does not print

Visit London says tipping is not mandatory. Its glance table lists hotel housekeeping as optional, £1 to £5 per night. A later hotel paragraph says you can leave an amount of your choosing for room-cleaning staff on departure. Both sentences are on that page. This guide does not merge them into "always £1 to £5, but only at checkout." If you use the table, three nights at £2 is £6, and three nights at £5 is £15. If you use the later sentence, the amount is the one you choose, and the timing it names is departure rather than each morning. The page does not explain the difference.

VisitBritain does not print a housekeeping figure. It says a porter who takes luggage to the room is customarily tipped about £2, and that a room-service tip is at your discretion, with about 10 percent appreciated if you are in doubt. Ten percent of a £20 room-service tray is £2. That 10 percent is VisitBritain's room-service doubt case. It is not a housekeeper rate, and it is not the Emily Post 15 to 20 percent for room service. Restaurant service charges in Britain are the UK guide.

Japan and Mexico

The Japan National Tourism Organization says tipping is not common for hotel service, along with bars, cafes, restaurants, and taxis. Leaving the U.S. daily envelope as a default fights that sentence. The page's exception is a private guide or interpreter, which is the tour guide guide, not the room cleaner. The longer Japan note is tipping in Japan.

Mexico's tourism secretariat FAQ says tipping is common and not mandatory, that 10 to 15 percent is common in restaurants, and that small tips are appreciated for hotel housekeeping and tour guides. It does not print a peso amount for the room. VisitMexico says a few coins are usually given to porters, cab drivers, and cleaning staff, and that 10 to 15 percent is the custom for meals or drinks in restaurants, bars, and street stands. "A few coins" is not 10 percent of the room. The meal range stays on the Mexico guide.

Pages that do not set a housekeeping amount

Tourism Australia's key facts say hotels and restaurants do not add a service charge, tipping is your choice, it is not expected in most venues, and 10 percent of the bill for good service is common in upmarket restaurants. The page does not mention housekeeping. This guide will not turn the restaurant 10 percent into a room-cleaner tip.

The VisitKorea food page says tipping is not required or expected in Korea, in a section about restaurants, water, and side dishes. It does not discuss a hotel room. Copying that restaurant sentence onto a housekeeper, or refusing to copy it, would both be a guess. The restaurant note stays on the South Korea guide.

Hotel housekeeping FAQ

Do I leave the Emily Post chart amount and the 2025 amount?

No. The chart is $2 to $5 per day. The February 6, 2025 note is roughly $2 to $3 per person per day. Use one count. Two people for three nights at $3 per person is $18, left across the mornings, not $18 plus another $2 to $5 per day.

Is the room's tax or resort fee the tip?

No. Québec's 3.5 percent accommodation tax is a tax. A room-service fee is excluded from the Emily Post room-service percent. A housekeeper tip on these pages is the cash amount the source actually prints, or, where the source prints none, no amount from this site.
