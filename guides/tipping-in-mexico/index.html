Tipping in Mexico

By Matt Totty, TipSlice · Last reviewed October 19, 2026

The tourism secretariat says tipping is common and not mandatory, and that 10% to 15% is common in restaurants. VisitMexico says 10% to 15% is customary for meals and drinks in restaurants, bars, and street stands. Profeco says a tip cannot be added to the bill without consent.

How much do the tourism pages say?

The secretariat FAQ answers “Should I tip in Mexico?” with one national sentence: although tipping is a common practice, it is not mandatory. It is common to tip 10% to 15% in restaurants. Small tips are appreciated for hotel housekeeping and tour guides. The page does not print a separate percent for Mexico City, Guadalajara, or Cancún, and it does not print a peso amount for a housekeeper or a guide.

VisitMexico answers the same question with “Yes.” It is customary to tip between 10% and 15% of the cost of meals or drinks in restaurants, bars, and at street stands or similar food sales. A few coins are usually given to porters, cab drivers, and cleaning staff. That page does not repeat the secretariat’s “not mandatory.”

What do the pages say about the bill and cash?

Profeco’s May 20, 2026 note says a tip cannot be demanded, that it is voluntary, and that it cannot be included on the bill without the diner’s consent. Restaurants and bars must display prices clearly, including taxes, in national currency, even if they also show dollars. They must hand over the receipt at the time of consumption. They must say which forms of payment they take, and they must accept bills of any denomination. The consumer phones on that page are 55 5568 8722 and 800 468 8722.

The two tourism pages do not use the same sentence for dollars. VisitMexico says Mexican pesos are the only acceptable currency, that carrying some pesos in cash matters for smaller day-to-day expenses, and that accepting U.S. dollars is widely discouraged. The secretariat FAQ says the peso is the official currency, that dollars are accepted in many tourist areas, and that exchanging currency is recommended for better rates. A later answer on that same FAQ says dollars are widely accepted in tourist areas, hotels, and many businesses, that change may come back in pesos, and that paying in pesos is often better. Those two currency answers stay side by side.

Mexico tipping FAQ

How much do people tip in a Mexican restaurant?

The tourism secretariat says tipping is common and not mandatory, and that 10% to 15% is common in restaurants. VisitMexico says 10% to 15% is customary for meals or drinks in restaurants, bars, and street stands. Profeco says a tip is voluntary and cannot be added to the bill without consent.

Why does the calculator start at 10% rather than 15%?

Ten percent is the low end of the 10% to 15% range both tourism pages print. Mexico City, Guadalajara, and Cancún share that start. You can type 15.

Can the restaurant print the tip, and can I pay in dollars?

Profeco says a tip cannot be demanded and cannot be included on the bill without the diner's consent. Prices must be shown in national currency, including taxes, and the restaurant must accept bills of any denomination. VisitMexico says pesos are the only acceptable currency and that accepting dollars is widely discouraged. The secretariat FAQ says dollars are accepted in many tourist areas.
