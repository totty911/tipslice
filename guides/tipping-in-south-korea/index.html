Tipping in South Korea

By Matt Totty, TipSlice · Last reviewed October 19, 2026

The Korea Tourism Organization’s English site, VisitKorea, says it directly in its guide to Korean food. In the section on visiting a Korean restaurant: tipping is not required or expected in Korea. Requests for more water or side dishes do not incur additional costs. The page, About Korean Food, says it was last updated on March 24, 2023, and that information may differ from what is presented there. That date is part of using the source. A custom described in 2023 can be revised. Until VisitKorea revises the sentence, it is the sentence this guide uses. A later VisitKorea food page, About Korean Food (the copy dated July 25, 2025 at the top), says the same thing in shorter words: at Korean restaurants, there's no need to tip, and extra water or side dishes don't add a charge. That page's footer still prints the March 24, 2023 update line. Both URLs are VisitKorea. Neither prints a percent.

Why the field starts at zero

Travel Tipper marks South Korea as not customary. Seoul, Busan, and Incheon all receive that status. The tip field goes to 0%, and a warning on the form repeats the VisitKorea line in plain language: a tip is not required or expected. The warning is there so a familiar 18% or 20% from home is not applied in silence. It is not a claim that offering money is an insult. VisitKorea does not say that. It says a tip is not required or expected. You can still type a percent if you want to leave something. The override is yours. The source link stays on the note either way.

The water-and-side-dish sentence is easy to miss and useful at the table. Asking for more banchan or more water is not, on this page, a request that adds a fee. You do not need to “cover” that refill with a tip for it to be allowed. The same section describes restaurants where people serve themselves water and side dishes, and others that use unmanned order machines. Those details are about how the room works, not about a percentage.

Won, not a percentage of a U.S. habit

The currency on those three cities is the won. A bill of 40,000 won at the starting 0% is still 40,000 won. Two people are 20,000 won each. The link is Seoul, 40,000 won, two people, 0%. If you type 10 anyway, the tip is 4,000 won and the total is 44,000 won. That 10% is your override. It is not a figure VisitKorea printed. Busan and Incheon open the same note; only the city name changes.

A home-currency total uses the currency converter rate table and names the source and time. Card networks and foreign-transaction fees can post a different amount. A terminal that wants to charge you in your home currency is a different rate again. Compare them before you accept that conversion.

A U.S. sit-down meal is the other setting. The restaurant guide cites Emily Post’s 15% to 20% before tax for that meal, next to U.S. wage rules that do not travel with you. Applying that range in Seoul because the card machine looks familiar ignores the VisitKorea sentence. Japan is the other not-customary country on Travel Tipper. JNTO says tipping is not common. Korea's sentence is the VisitKorea line: not required or expected.

What to do with the check

Read the total you were given. If it matches the dishes, the VisitKorea note says you are not required to add a tip. If a charge is printed that you do not understand, ask what it is before you add a percent on top. This guide does not have a Korean tax table, so Travel Tipper does not fill one. The tax field stays at 0% until you type a rate that is actually on the bill.

The VisitKorea page itself warns that it may be out of date relative to its March 24, 2023 update. Treat that as the caveat. Customs and restaurant software change. The receipt is the amount you were charged. This page is not legal advice, and it is not a promise that a particular restaurant still follows the 2023 note.

South Korea tipping FAQ

Do restaurants in South Korea expect a tip?

The Korea Tourism Organization says tipping is not required or expected in Korea. The same note says requests for more water or side dishes do not add a charge.

Can I still leave a tip if I want to?

Yes. Travel Tipper sets Seoul, Busan, and Incheon to 0% and shows the VisitKorea note, and you can type a different percent. The source does not describe a required amount.
