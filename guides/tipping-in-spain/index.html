Tipping in Spain

By Matt Totty, TipSlice · Last reviewed October 19, 2026

A tip in Spain is not obligatory. spain.info, the national tourism site, says establishments include the service charge. It is still common to tip in bars, restaurants, hotels, and taxis. The amount depends on the bill and the guest, and it usually varies between 5% and 10% of the total.

Do you have to tip in Spain?

No. The heading on the tourism page is "Tips. Do you need to give tips? Is it recommended?" The answer is the sentence above: not obligatory, because the service charge is included, and common in those four settings. The page does not split the 5% to 10% by city, by bar versus dining room, or by whether you pay cash. Madrid, Barcelona, and Seville are not given separate percents.

The same page says that when you pay, it is not acceptable to haggle the marked price. A tip is not a negotiation over the menu. It is an amount on top of a price that already includes service, if you choose to leave one.

What does servicio incluido refer to?

Diners use "servicio incluido" for service that is already in the price. The English tourism page does not print those Spanish words, and it does not say a menu must carry that phrase or a stated percent. What it does say is that all establishments include the service charge, which is why a tip is not obligatory. This site's Travel Tipper label, "service usually included," is a short name for that sentence. It is not a second Spanish law.

France's consumer page does require the words "prix service compris" and a percentage where service is charged. Spain's tourism page does not copy that rule, so this guide does not borrow it. Read the bill for a line that is already service. If that line is there, the 5% to 10% is not a second charge you owe on top of it.

Where does the 5% to 10% apply?

On a €40 total, 5% is €2.00 and 10% is €4.00. The totals are €42.00 and €44.00. Those two figures are the ends of the range the tourism page calls usual. They are not a bill you have to pay. The links open them only if you are choosing to leave that percent: Madrid, €40, two people, 0%, the same bill at 5%, and at 10%.

Why does Travel Tipper start at 0%?

Madrid, Barcelona, and Seville use the country note. The tip field starts at 0% so a percent is not stacked on service the tourism page says is already included. The note still tells you the 5% to 10% range. Type one of those after you have read the bill. The currency is the euro. spain.info says one euro is 100 cents, notes run from €5 to €500, and cards, mainly Visa and Mastercard, are widespread. A card payment may require a minimum around €10 and may carry a commission set by the card, not by this page.

A home-currency total uses the currency converter's mid-market table and names the source and time. It is not the amount a card terminal will post if it offers to charge your home currency. The wider chart, including countries with a different rule, is tipping when traveling.
