Tipping in Italy

By Matt Totty, TipSlice · Last updated October 17, 2026

Italy’s own tourism site is the source for this page. Italia.it, on its page about money, payments, and tipping, answers “Tipping in Italy: yes or no?” in one paragraph. Tipping is not compulsory. There are no established rules. It is customary to leave an amount close to 10% of the bill when the customer is satisfied with the service received. That is the whole tipping rule the page states. TipSlice does not add a Rome percent, a Milan percent, or a Florence percent on top of it.

What the sentence does and does not say

“Not compulsory” is why Travel Tipper’s status for Italy is optional, and why Rome, Milan, and Florence start at 0%. A box that opened at 10% would treat a customary thank-you as a default charge. The same sentence still gives you the amount people leave when they are satisfied: close to 10% of the bill. If that is what you want, type 10. If the meal was ordinary and you are following the “not compulsory” half of the sentence, leave the zero. The page does not say a tip is rude, and it does not say service is already a fixed percentage inside the menu price. Those claims belong to other countries’ sources. They are not in this Italia.it paragraph, so they are not in this guide.

The same Italia.it page says the currency is the euro, and it lists coin and note denominations. It also says receipts and bills are obligatory: businesses must issue a payment slip, and you should ask for it and keep it. That is a payment rule, not a tip rule, but it matters at the table. The slip is where you see whether anything besides the dishes was added. If a charge is printed and you did not choose it, read it before you add a second percent. This guide does not invent a name for that charge, because the tipping paragraph does not describe one.

Italia.it also says displayed prices are the prices, and that haggling is not the general custom, with an exception for fairs and markets. A blank tip line is not a negotiation over the menu price. It is the optional amount in the tipping paragraph, close to 10% when you are satisfied, or nothing when you are not adding one.

One country, three cities

Travel Tipper lists Rome, Milan, and Florence because they are major cities where a visitor is likely to have a bill, not because each has its own custom. Selecting any of them sets the currency to euros, sets the tip field to 0%, and shows the Italia.it note with the link. You can change the percent after that. A share link can lock the percent you chose. A dinner for two that you want left as the bill alone is Rome, €64, two people, 0%. The same bill with the customary satisfied-guest figure is Rome, €64, two people, 10%. Ten percent of €64 is €6.40. The total is €70.40, and each person’s share rounds up so the two shares cover the check.

If the euro total needs to be in dollars, the home-currency line uses the same mid-market table as the currency converter. That line names its source and time. It is not the amount your card will post. A terminal that offers to charge your home currency is using a different rate. Compare the two before you accept the terminal’s conversion.

Do not import a U.S. 20%

A sit-down tip in the United States is a different custom. The restaurant guide cites Emily Post’s 15% to 20% of the pre-tax bill for that setting. Italia.it does not cite that range, and it says there are no established rules. Using 20% because it is familiar is a choice you can still type. It is not what the Italian tourism page describes. The broader comparison, including countries where service is already in the price, is tipping when traveling.

Tourism pages are revised on their own schedule. If a check in your hand disagrees with this summary, pay the check. This page is a worksheet for the Italia.it paragraph, not a local ordinance and not tax advice.

Italy tipping FAQ

Is a tip required in Italy?

Italia.it says tipping in Italy is not compulsory and there are no established rules. It also says it is customary to leave an amount close to 10% of the bill when the customer is satisfied with the service.

Why does Travel Tipper start Rome at 0%?

Because the national tourism site says a tip is not compulsory. The note still tells you that close to 10% is customary when you are satisfied, and you can type that percent. Rome, Milan, and Florence share the same country note.
